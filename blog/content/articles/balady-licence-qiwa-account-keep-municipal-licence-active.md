Businesses in Saudi Arabia are being reminded that their **Balady municipal licence** has to stay active and match their establishment record. If it doesn't, the warnings say, the company's **Qiwa** services can be restricted and day-to-day operations disrupted. These include work permit issuance and renewals, visas and worker transfers.

We could not find an official Ministry of Human Resources or Ministry of Municipalities and Housing announcement that sets out a new "link your Balady licence to Qiwa" rule. Even so, the risk is real. A municipal licence that lapses is suspended, and later cancelled, under rules already in force. And Saudi government platforms increasingly check each other's records automatically. This article explains what is confirmed, what is reported, and what to check.

---

## 1. What Balady and Qiwa Each Do

| Platform | Run By | What It Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Balady** (balady.gov.sa) | Ministry of Municipalities and Housing | Municipal (commercial) licences for premises and activities, renewals, suspensions, cancellations, craft licences |
| **Qiwa** (qiwa.sa) | Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development | Establishment labour file: work permits, visas, worker transfers, contracts, Nitaqat |

The two platforms are already connected in at least one area. Balady's **craft licence** service checks through its **integration with Qiwa** that the worker has passed the required professional exam, and it needs the worker's data to be on Qiwa.

---

## 2. What the Municipal Licensing Rules Say About Expiry

The **Implementing Regulations of the Law of Municipal Licensing Procedures** (published in Umm Al-Qura) set out what happens around a commercial licence's expiry date:

| Stage | What Happens |
| --- | --- |
| **30 days before expiry** | The municipality notifies the licence holder |
| **Before expiry** | The holder should request renewal, cancellation or suspension, to avoid renewal fees being charged automatically |
| **Renewal requirements** | A valid establishment document (commercial register / unified number), Civil Defence approval, and payment of fees |
| **After expiry** | Late payment of renewal fees attracts a fine |
| **90 days after expiry without renewal** | The commercial licence is **cancelled automatically** |

The Regulations also separate two outcomes that are often confused:

- **Suspension (إيقاف):** the municipality stops the activity, but the licence stays valid until the reasons for suspension are removed. The municipality can close the premises while it is suspended.
- **Cancellation (إلغاء):** the licence is annulled. Grounds include non-renewal, a municipal violation, a reasoned request from another government authority, or the owner's own request after paying all dues.

Commercial licences can be issued for **1 to 5 years**. Health-related, hazardous and service-centre activities are limited to **1 year**. If your licence's renewal date differs from your commercial register and other renewals, it can lapse without anyone noticing.

---

## 3. How This Reaches Your Qiwa Account

Several Saudi compliance consultancies report that Qiwa now checks the **municipal licence of the establishment's premises**. Where the licence has expired, or there is no active documented location, they report that **work permit issuance and renewal for that branch's workers can be suspended** until the licence is renewed and the establishment's data is updated.

We have not seen this confirmed in an official ministry announcement, so treat it as a reported practice, not a published rule. It matches what we see across government platforms: each one checks the others, and when one record lapses, services on another platform can stop.

It also matters because Qiwa itself is enforcing deadlines more strictly. Since **1 July 2026**, Qiwa has automatically removed resident employees from an establishment's records when their work permits have been **expired for more than three months**. The employer stays responsible for obligations up to the removal date. A municipal licence problem that blocks permit renewals can therefore lead to workers being removed from your records.

## What Businesses Should Do Now

1. **Check every municipal licence on Balady.** Log in as the owner or establishment manager and use the *Inquiry about Establishment Licences* service. Note the status and expiry date of each one.
2. **Confirm each licence is tied to the correct establishment.** The commercial register / unified number, the National Address and the branch should match your records on Qiwa and with the Ministry of Commerce.
3. **Renew before expiry, not after.** Renewal needs Civil Defence approval, which takes time. You only have a short window after expiry before fines apply, and 90 days before cancellation.
4. **Watch for multi-branch gaps.** Each branch with premises has its own licence. One expired branch licence can block permit renewals for that branch's workers.
5. **Clear outstanding municipal fines and violations.** Unpaid fines and repeated violations are grounds for suspension or cancellation.
6. **Check your Qiwa establishment page.** Look for any alerts or blocked services, and fix them before a work permit or visa request is due.
7. **Put every renewal date on one compliance calendar**, including municipal licences, commercial register confirmation, Civil Defence, work permits and Iqamas. Licences most often lapse when people assume these dates fall together.

Municipal licences, Civil Defence approvals and Qiwa permits are handled by different ministries on different timelines, so they easily fall out of step.
